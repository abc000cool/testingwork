Deep Sea Facts

Five things about the darkest place on Earth.

The midnight zone

Below about 1,000 meters, sunlight never reaches. This zone makes up the majority of the ocean's volume, yet we've mapped less of it than the surface of Mars.

Most of it glows

An estimated 75% of deep-sea animals produce their own light — used for luring prey, confusing predators, and finding mates in total darkness.

Pressure is enormous

At the bottom of the Mariana Trench, pressure reaches roughly 1,100 atmospheres — about the weight of 50 jumbo jets pressing on a person.

Life without sun

Hydrothermal vent ecosystems run on chemosynthesis instead of photosynthesis, with bacteria turning hydrogen sulfide into energy.

Very slow living

Cold and scarce food mean slow metabolisms. Some deep-sea corals and tube worms are believed to live for several centuries.
